# Saakshi field kit: interviews, 12–14 Nov 2026

**Purpose.** In three days, find out four things:
1. Do jewellers and karigars really live with the problem in statement 2.2?
2. Would a shared, voice-first record get used?
3. Who would pay?
4. What numbers, stories and photos can go straight into the 15 Nov submission?

**How to use this kit.**
- Read it once tonight.
- Print the **consent card** (§3) and the **pocket card** (last page).
- Go in pairs: one person asks, the other writes.
- **Interview karigars away from their jeweller.**

**Three rules for every interview**
1. **Ask about the last real time, never the future.** "Tell me about the last time you gave gold to a karigar" beats "Would you use…?"
2. **Collect numbers and stories, not opinions.** Compliments are not data. Money or effort already spent on the problem is data.
3. **Pitch only at the end.** Show the concept after you understand how things work today (§8).

The six official field questions from the statement are tagged **[PS-Q1] … [PS-Q6]** wherever they are covered.

---

## 1. What we must learn

The thresholds assume about 10 jewellers and 10 karigars. Scale them down if you meet fewer.

| # | Hypothesis | What tells us | Passes if |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Disputes or unexplained shortfalls are common | Jeweller recalls a weight, purity, wastage or payment mismatch in the last 6 months | ≥6/10 jewellers |
| H2 | It costs real money or time | Grams lost, time spent on *hisab* and chasing, an absconding story known in the market | Median ≥1 g a month **or** ≥3 h a week; ≥1 absconding story per market |
| H3 | Today's record is one-sided | Karigar holds no copy they control (or only a loose slip) | ≥7/10 karigars |
| H4 | Karigars want a shared record and will confirm it by voice or tap | Reaction after hearing the mock voice note, and whether they understood it | ≥6/10 say yes **and** ≥8/10 restate the weight and date correctly |
| H5 | The channels work | WhatsApp use, voice notes, UPI, phone type | ≥8/10 karigars on WhatsApp; ≥6/10 use voice notes |
| H6 | Capture can be fast | How long an issue entry takes today; scale display legible; scale model | Entry takes ≥30 s today; displays photograph cleanly |
| H7 | Deadlines slip | Late custom or wedding orders last season; how they were chased | ≥5/10 jewellers had one [PS-Q3] |
| H8 | Karigars wait to be paid | Days from delivery to payment | Median ≥14 days [PS-Q4] |
| H9 | Someone will pay and commit | Reaction to price; agrees to a December pilot with ≥2 karigars | ≥4/10 jewellers agree to pilot; ≥3/10 call ₹999/month "fair" or "cheap" |
| H10 | Karigars need credit and would share their record | Recent informal borrowing; willingness to share history with a lender or new jeweller | ≥5/10 karigars |

**Kill and pivot rules (decide on Friday night)**
- **H1 and H3 both fail** → no problem, or no gap. Stop and rethink the problem.
- **H4 fails** (karigars refuse or are afraid) → keep the jeweller-side fine-gold ledger and the deadline agent, make the karigar side optional, then re-test.
- **H9 fails but H1–H4 pass** → the problem is real but the buyer is wrong. Probe wholesalers, manufacturers, insurers and associations as payers.

## 2. Who to meet, and where

**Quotas (minimum for a credible submission)**

| Who | How many | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Retail jewellers who make custom or wedding orders | 6–8 | Core buyer |
| Wholesalers or manufacturers who issue gold to many karigars | 1–2 | Bigger buyer, many karigars at once |
| Karigars (workers) | 6–8 | Core user; fairness |
| Head karigars (*babu*) or workshop owners | 2–3 | Chain of custody; they hold the most gold |
| Hallmarking centre (AHC) staff | 1 | Purity failures, data |
| XRF or "touch" testing shop | 1 | Candidate neutral verification point |
| Refiner or dust (*dhool*) buyer | 1 | What wastage really is |
| Jewellers' or karigar welfare association office-bearer | 1 | Distribution, trust, absconding |
| CA who files jewellers' GST (phone is fine) | 1 | Job-work challans, ITC-04 |
| JanAI Café, CSC or similar operator (phone is fine) | 1 | Assisted path, fees |

**Jewellery clusters**
- **Bengaluru** (also the finale city): Avenue Road, Chickpet, Nagarthpet.
- **Chennai:** Sowcarpet, NSC Bose Road, T. Nagar.
- **Mumbai:** Zaveri Bazaar.
- **Kolkata:** Bowbazar, Sinthi More, and the Ankurhati jewellery park in Domjur (Howrah).
- **Delhi:** Chandni Chowk, Karol Bagh.
- **Hyderabad:** around Charminar.
- **Meerut:** 30,000+ Bengali artisans, according to the bullion traders' association.

Large Bengali karigar communities also work in Coimbatore, Thrissur and other South Indian hubs.

**How to get in**
1. **Start from someone you know.** Any jeweller you or your family know. Ask them to introduce two karigars, then meet them separately.
2. **Go to a hallmarking centre or a testing shop.** Every registered jeweller sends pieces there, and the staff know who's who.
3. **Try the associations.** Local jewellers' associations, bullion associations, or Bengali *swarnakar* welfare associations.
4. **Ask bullion dealers.** They supply most jewellers in a market.

**Timing**
- **Jewellers:** visit 10:30–13:00 and 15:00–17:30. Avoid evenings, which are peak footfall, and check each market's weekly holiday.
- **Karigars:** they work 14 h a day on average, and more than 16 h in October–December ([IIMA/XLRI study](#sources)). Keep their interviews to 20 minutes, go at tea or meal breaks, and offer to come back.
- **This week is right after Diwali (8 Nov).** Some Bengali karigars may still be at home. Ask for their phone numbers and do a follow-up call.

## 3. Ethics, consent and safety (non-negotiable)

- **Tell the truth about who you are.** You are students in a hackathon. You are not from police, tax, BIS or any bank, and you are not selling anything.
- **Get verbal consent before any questions. Record only with explicit permission.** Without it, take written notes only.
- **Use codes, not names**: J01, K01, A01 for the AHC, and so on. Never write down:
  - a person's name next to the gold they hold;
  - workshop addresses;
  - phone numbers in the notes file. Keep those separately, only if they agreed to a follow-up.
- **No photos of faces, gold stock, safes, CCTV or shop signage.** Scale-display photos follow §9.
- **Interview karigars without the jeweller present.** Never repeat what one side told you to the other.
- **Don't dig into cash or tax practices.** If someone volunteers it, don't record specifics.
- **Promise nothing**: no loans, no jobs, no fixes.
- **Stay safe.** Go in pairs, carry no valuables, and be discreet with phones in bullion lanes.
- **Store notes in a private team folder.** Delete raw audio once it has been transcribed, unless the person agreed to it being used for product testing (§9).

## 4. Jeweller interview (35–45 min)

### A. Context (5 min)
1. How long has the shop been running? What do you mostly sell: ready stock, custom orders or wedding sets?
2. Of what you sell, roughly how much do outside karigars make, how much do you buy ready-made, and how much is made in-house?
3. **How many karigars do you give gold to these days? How many orders are open right now?** [PS-Q6]
4. Who handles karigar work: you, family, a manager, or counter staff?

### B. The last job, step by step (10 min) [PS-Q1]
5. **"Tell me about the last time you gave gold to a karigar. What was it for?"** Probe each point:
   - How did you choose that karigar?
   - How much gold, at what purity, and in what form (bar, old gold, 22K)?
   - Who weighed it, on which scale? Did the karigar weigh it too?
   - What was written down, and where? Did the karigar sign anything or take anything away: a slip, a photo, a message?
   - Were wastage and making charge agreed then, out loud or in writing?
   - How was the due date chosen? Was it tied to a customer's occasion?
6. Since then, how have you followed up? How many calls?
7. When it comes back: who weighs it, how is purity checked (own XRF, touchstone, outside shop), and how is the balance worked out? *Ask them to show you on a blank page.*
8. How and when do you pay the karigar: per job or at month end, cash or UPI?

### C. Numbers (5 min)
9. Roughly how many grams go out to karigars in a normal month? In wedding season?
10. **What wastage do you allow for plain 22K? For studded work? Who decided it?** [PS-Q2]
    - Is the percentage taken on the gold you issue or on what comes back?
    - Is it on gross weight or on fine gold?
11. How much time a day do you or your staff spend on karigar *hisab* and follow-up?
12. Out of 10 custom orders, how many come back late? [PS-Q3]

### D. When things go wrong (10 min) [PS-Q2, Q3, Q5]
13. **"When was the last time the numbers didn't match?"** Was it weight, purity, wastage or payment? How much? How was it settled? How did the relationship feel afterwards?
14. When the karigar's number differs from yours, whose number wins, and why? [PS-Q5]
15. Has a piece ever failed at the hallmarking centre? What did you do? Who paid for the rework?
16. **"The last time a wedding order was late, what happened with the customer?"** [PS-Q3]
17. How do you decide how much gold a new karigar can be trusted with? Has anyone in this market lost gold to a karigar who disappeared? *(Gently; no names.)*
18. Have you ever been in a disagreement where you wished you had proof?

### E. Today's tools and spend (5 min)
19. What do you use today: a notebook, slips, Tally or another ERP, Excel, WhatsApp photos, Khatabook or OkCredit? Can I see the format? *(A blank page is enough.)*
20. What do you pay today for software or an accountant? What do you like and dislike about it?
21. Do you issue delivery challans for job work? Who handles ITC-04, you or your CA?
22. Does your insurance cover gold lying with karigars? What records does the insurer ask for?

### F. Concept test (5–7 min, only now; see §8)
23. Show the 3-step mock: scale photo → karigar voice note (play it) → karigar says "haan" → both get the same receipt. Then ask: **"What would change in your shop if every issue worked like this?"**
24. "What would stop you using it? What would stop your karigars?"
25. "If it saved you *[repeat their own number from C or D]*, what would that be worth? Would ₹999 a month be cheap, fair or expensive?"
26. **The commitment ask:** "We're running a free two-week pilot in December. Would you try it with two of your karigars? When can we come back?"

### G. Close (1 min)
27. "Who else should we talk to: a karigar, another jeweller, the hallmarking centre?"
28. Thank them. Ask permission to message them on WhatsApp later.

## 5. Karigar interview (20–30 min)

### A. Context (4 min)
1. How long have you done this work? Where did you learn it? Where is home? *(District is optional.)*
2. Do you work alone, or in a workshop? Is there a head karigar (*babu*)?
3. **How many jewellers or shops do you work for these days?**
4. What kind of work do you do: handmade, casting, setting, polishing, bangles, chains?
5. Which languages do you speak? Which do you read? What phone do you have? Do you use WhatsApp, voice notes, UPI?

### B. The last gold you received (8 min) [PS-Q1, Q2]
6. **"Tell me about the last time you received gold."** From whom, how much, and who weighed it? Did you weigh it on your own scale too?
7. Did you get anything in writing: a slip, a photo, a message? Who keeps the record?
8. How were wastage (*ghat*) and labour (*majuri*) agreed? Written down, or spoken?
9. When was it due? Did they call to follow up? How many times?

### C. Return and payment (6 min) [PS-Q4]
10. When you returned it, how was it weighed and tested? Did you both agree on the numbers?
11. **After you delivered, how many days did it take to get paid?** Cash or UPI? Paid in full, or in parts?
12. Is any gold or money pending between you and a jeweller right now? Roughly how long has it been pending? *(No names.)*

### D. When the numbers differ (6 min) [PS-Q5]
13. **"When the jeweller's number differs from yours, what do you do?"**
14. The last time it happened, how big was the difference? How was it settled? Did it feel fair?
15. Has a piece of yours ever failed at hallmarking? Who paid for the rework?
16. If you needed ₹50,000 tomorrow, where would you get it, and at what interest? Has a bank ever asked about your work?
17. When a jeweller gives you gold for the first time, how much do they trust you with? How did that grow over time?

### E. Concept test (5 min; see §8)
18. Play the voice note in their language. Ask: **"What did it say?"** Note whether they got the weight, purity, item and date right.
19. "Would you say *haan* to this? Would you want this record on your phone? Why, or why not?"
20. **"Is there anything about this that worries you?"** Probe for these:
    - the jeweller using it against them;
    - privacy;
    - tax or police;
    - being judged on wastage.
21. "If you could show a new jeweller, or a bank, a record of all the work you've delivered on time, would that help? Would you share it?"
22. "Would you try this in December with your jeweller?"

### F. Close
23. "Who else should we talk to?" Thank them.

## 6. Head karigar or workshop owner add-on (10 min)
- How many karigars work under you? How do you hand out gold to them, and do they confirm it in any way? How do you track who holds what?
- What wastage do you get from jewellers, and what do you allow your karigars? Who absorbs a loss?
- How does work reach you: directly from jewellers, through vendors or wholesalers, or through other head karigars?
- What is the biggest risk you carry: theft, a karigar leaving, disputes, delays?
- Would tracking the gold you pass on to your own karigars help you? Would you pay for it, or would the jeweller?

## 7. Short guides (10–15 min each)

**Hallmarking centre (AHC)**
- How many articles a day?
- Roughly what share of lots fail purity at first test, and why: solder, under-alloying, KDM marks, hollow or filled pieces?
- What happens to a failed piece, and how does the jeweller resubmit? Do jewellers go back to the karigar? With what evidence?
- BIS now captures XRF readings and weight automatically. Can jewellers download per-piece results?
- Would you act as a neutral re-test point in jeweller–karigar disputes? At what fee?

**XRF or touch testing shop**
- Who comes in: jewellers, karigars, customers?
- How often is it for a jeweller–karigar disagreement?
- How far apart are two machines on the same piece? How far is XRF from fire assay?
- What does a test cost?
- Would you act as a neutral "re-weigh and re-test" point, for a fee per service?

**Refiner or dust (*dhool*) buyer**
- How much gold comes back from workshop dust and sweepings per kg processed?
- Who sells the dust, the karigar or the jeweller, and who keeps the money?
- Do you issue any record? Would a recovery certificate linked to the karigar's account be useful?

**Association office-bearer**
- How many members do you have? How often do absconding or dispute cases come to you? What do you do today: verification, guarantees, WhatsApp alerts?
- Would you endorse a pilot or share it with members? What would worry you about it: privacy, misuse, cost?

**CA**
- How do your jeweller clients handle job-work challans and ITC-04 today? What is painful about it?
- Would a clean export save you time? How many such clients do you have?

**JanAI Café, CSC or similar operator**
- Do karigar families come to you?
- Which services would they pay for: scheme forms, remittance, insurance?
- Could you onboard jewellers or karigars for a fee? What fee per service feels fair?

## 8. Concept test (end of the interview only)

**Prepare tonight**
1. **Mock voice notes, about 15 seconds each**, in Bengali, Hindi, Kannada and Tamil. Have a native speaker record them, or assemble them from recorded phrases the way Saakshi's receipts will be built (testing that voice is part of the point). The scripts are below.
2. **A receipt card image**, phone-sized, with big digits:
   > 48.250 g · 22K (916) · 2 bangles · Due 10 Dec · Lakshmi Jewellers · Ref SKS-7F3A · ✅ Confirmed by both
3. **A one-screen mock of the jeweller's view**: karigars, gold out with each, due dates, a red "late" flag. Paper sketch or Figma.
4. **A karigar "passport" card**: 42 jobs · 3.1 kg handled · 95% on time · within agreed wastage 40/42 · 0 open disputes.

**Mock voice-note scripts** (native speakers must check them)
- **Hindi:** "Namaste Rafiq bhai. Lakshmi Jewellers ne aaj aapko 22 carat ka 48 gram 250 milligram sona diya hai, do kangan ke liye. Delivery 10 December. Sahi hai toh 'haan' boliye, nahi toh 'nahi' boliye."
- **Bengali:** "নমস্কার রফিক ভাই। লক্ষ্মী জুয়েলার্স আজ আপনাকে ২২ ক্যারেটের ৪৮ গ্রাম ২৫০ মিলিগ্রাম সোনা দিয়েছে, দুটো চুড়ির জন্য। ডেলিভারি ১০ ডিসেম্বর। ঠিক থাকলে 'হ্যাঁ' বলুন, না হলে 'না' বলুন।" *(Nomoskar Rafiq bhai. Lakshmi Jewellers aaj apnake 22 carat-er 48 gram 250 milligram sona diyeche, duto churir jonno. Delivery 10 December. Thik thakle 'hyan' bolun, na hole 'na' bolun.)*
- **Kannada:** "Namaskara Rafiq avare. Lakshmi Jewellers indu nimage 22 carat-ina 48 gram 250 milligram chinna kottiddare, eradu balegalige. Delivery Disembar 10. Sari iddare 'haudu' anta heli, illadiddare 'illa' anta heli."
- **Tamil:** "Vanakkam Rafiq. Lakshmi Jewellers indru ungalukku 22 carat 48 gram 250 milligram thangam koduthirukkaanga, rendu valaiyalukkaaga. Delivery December 10. Sari-na 'aamaa' sollunga, illa-na 'illai' sollunga."

**Protocol**
1. Play the voice note once. Ask "What did it say?" Score 0–4 for weight, purity, item and date.
2. Ask for their reaction, then **"What would stop you?"**
3. Ask for a commitment: a December pilot, or an introduction.
4. **Don't defend the idea.** Write objections down word for word; they are the most valuable thing you'll collect.

## 9. Observe and collect

**Observation checklist (per shop or workshop)**
- [ ] **Scale:** brand and model; readability (0.001 or 0.01 g); display type (LCD or LED, backlight colour); any RS-232 or USB port; date on the Legal Metrology stamping sticker.
- [ ] **Purity testing:** does the shop own an XRF (which model)? A touchstone? Do they use an outside shop, and at what cost per test?
- [ ] **Records:** the notebook's columns, slips, who writes them, signatures. *Photo of a blank or redacted page, with permission.*
- [ ] **Phones:** karigar's phone model; WhatsApp, voice notes, UPI; languages heard on the shop floor.
- [ ] **Time and motion:** with permission, stopwatch one issue entry and one receipt entry. This is the baseline for "Saakshi takes 15 seconds."

**Scale-photo dataset** (it becomes our accuracy benchmark)
- **Permission:** ask first. Put a **non-gold object** on the pan, such as their test weight, a coin or keys. Never include customers, gold stock or faces.
- **Photos:** 15–30 per scale. Shoot straight on, from 30° left and right, close and far, with and without flash, and with glare.
- **Truth labels:** write the true displayed reading on the sheet. Name files `scale_<cluster>_<scaleID>_<reading>_<nn>.jpg`.
- **Target:** ≥300 photos across ≥10 scale models.

**Voice-sample set** (needs separate, explicit consent: "for testing voice features, deleted on request")
- **What to record:** ask people to say these phrases **in their own words and language**. Their natural phrasing is worth more than the exact script.
- **Jeweller style:**
  1. "Rafiq ko do kangan ke liye 48 gram 250 milli, 22 carat, das tareekh tak."
  2. "Salim, ek haar, 916, 62 gram, shaadi chaudah December ki hai."
  3. "Ghat chaar percent, majuri 450 rupaye gram."
  4. "Stone 32 piece, 1.8 carat diye hain."
  5. "Kal tak delivery chahiye, customer ka function hai."
- **Karigar style:**
  6. "Haan, sahi hai." / "Nahi, 48.200 hi mila."
  7. "Kaam chal raha hai, do din aur lagenge."
  8. "Polish mein ghat zyada gaya, ek gram do sau milli."
  9. "Mera hisab batao."
  10. "Payment kab milega?"
  11. "Tunch 91.6 hai, machine galat hai."
  12. "Teen piece wapas diye, ek baaki hai."
- **Bengali equivalents:** "হ্যাঁ, ঠিক আছে" (hyan, thik achhe) · "আরও দু'দিন লাগবে" (aro du'din lagbe) · "আমার হিসাবটা বলুন" (amar hisabta bolun) · "টাকা কবে পাব?" (taka kobe pabo?)
- **Also note** any local words for wastage, purity, labour and pieces. They go into Saakshi's vocabulary list.
- **Target:** ≥150 utterances from ≥20 speakers, across at least Bengali, Hindi and one southern language.

## 11. Daily synthesis (60 minutes every evening)
1. **Tally the scoreboard.** For each hypothesis, count the evidence: H1 __/__ … H10 __/__.
2. **Collect the top 10 quotes**, verbatim and coded, e.g. *K04: "Hisab unka, galti hamari."*
3. **Fill the numbers table.** Take medians of: karigars per jeweller, grams a month, wastage %, days to pay, late orders, time per entry.
4. **Answer four questions:**
   - What did we hear three or more times?
   - What surprised us?
   - What did we get wrong?
   - What changes in the solution?
5. **Friday night: decide.** Apply the kill and pivot rules (§1) and freeze the scope for the submission.

## Pocket card (print one per interviewer)

**JEWELLER: ask about the *last real time***
1. Pichhli baar karigar ko sona diya: kya banwana tha, kitna diya, kaise likha? *[Q1]*
2. Karigar ke paas kya record rehta hai? Sign karta hai, photo leta hai?
3. Ghat kaise tay hota hai? Plain 22K mein kitna, studded mein kitna? Likhit ya zubaani? Issue pe ya wapsi pe? *[Q2]*
4. Pichhle 6 mahine mein hisab kab match nahi hua? Kitna farak? Kaise suljha? *[Q5]*
5. Shaadi ka order late hua kabhi? Phir customer ke saath kya hua? *[Q3]*
6. Karigar ko maal ke baad kitne din mein payment? Cash ya UPI? *[Q4]*
7. Abhi kitne karigar, kitne open order? *[Q6]*
8. Naye karigar ko pehli baar kitna sona? Bharosa kaise badhta hai?
9. Hallmark centre mein piece fail hua? Kharcha kisne uthaya?
